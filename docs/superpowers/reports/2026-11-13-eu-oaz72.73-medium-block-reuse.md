# eu-oaz72.73: medium objects reuse swept blocks; footprint held to the heap limit

Date: 2026-11-13. Bead: eu-oaz72.73 (blocks eu-oaz72.4).

## Defect

`HeapState::replace_overflow` always took `BumpBlock::new()`. Medium objects
(more than a line, less than a block, e.g. set storage of ~1,000 elements)
never landed in swept space, so the heap grew with medium allocation volume
however little was live. `--heap-limit-mib` checked only measured live data,
so the growth went past the limit.

## Change

1. **Free-block pool.** Sweeping files a reclaimable block with no marked
   line at the back of `recycled`, and a partly used one at the front.
   `replace_overflow(size)` takes a free block (lazily sweeping `unswept`
   until one appears), then a recycled block whose largest hole fits (first
   8 only), and only then a fresh block.
2. **Footprint under a limit.** `policy_requires_collection` also fires once
   the blocks held (bump blocks plus large-object blocks) pass a footprint
   trigger: 7/8 of the limit, or halfway from the post-collection footprint
   to the tolerated footprint when fragmentation leaves more than 7/8 held,
   so there is always headroom. `defer_sweep` returns `recycled` to
   `unswept` after every collection, so each block's cursor and its place
   in the list follow the new marks. After each
   polled collection `Heap::enforce_footprint_limit` releases free blocks
   down to 3/4 of the limit. Two successive collections leaving more than the
   limit plus a tolerance (half of it, at least 1 MiB) raise
   `HeapLimitExceeded`. The tolerance is for fragmentation, which this
   collector cannot compact away. At a 2 MiB limit, the prelude and program
   data alone hold up to 75 blocks (the limit is 64) in blob mode. With live
   data at about 60% of a 16 MiB limit, benches 017 and 018 and harness test
   228 hold over 18 MiB. At 1/8 tolerance those three errored where master
   succeeded; at half the limit they pass. At 8 MiB they fail on master too.
3. **Bounded best-fit.** Head replacement's best-fit search
   (`find_best_recycled_block`) now scans the first 16 recycled blocks, not
   all. Without that, the eager sweep in step 1 made `recycled` long, and
   018_string_scale's mutator time rose from 0.11 s to 0.28 s.

The heap-limit error now reads "N MiB remains in use after garbage
collection", covering both live data and blocks held.

## Measurements (measured-single; release build, source prelude, `-S`)

Set-churn repro from the bead (`foldl` with `set.remove`/`set.add`,
`EU_PP_THRESHOLD=999999999`), peak heap blocks:

| n | master | this change |
|---|---|---|
| 4,000 | 3,765 | 1,477 |
| 16,000 | 17,451 | 5,876 |
| 32,000, `--heap-limit-mib 256` (8,192 blocks) | 34,638 | 7,728 |

The remaining growth with n is live data: that `foldl` accumulator is not
forced, so the stack holds about 4n frames (Max Stack 129,012 at n=32,000).

the peak does not depend on n: 1,030 / 1,031 / 1,031 blocks for n =
4k / 16k / 64k. Master: 26,063 blocks at n = 32k unbounded, and 25,976 under
`--heap-limit-mib 32` (1,024 blocks); this change, 901 blocks under 32 MiB.

## Open

- The non-strict `foldl` accumulator (Max Stack 4n) keeps live data growing
  with n in the bead's original repro. That is a strictness gap, not a GC
  one (eu-oaz72.27 / .34 territory).
- Evacuation candidate analysis looks only at head, overflow and `rest`
  blocks, never at `unswept` or `recycled`. So it cannot compact a heap that
  fragmentation holds over its limit, and the footprint check has to
  tolerate that instead.
